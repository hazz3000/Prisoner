Which ad should each person see next?

Each person in your audience will see up to a few of your ads. The only data kept is campaign totals: impressions and conversions per cohort and ad, nothing about any person. This page simulates each strategy for choosing those ads on the same audience, then shows how many conversions each one gets.

The crossed rotation

During the test phase, each new person in a cohort gets the next block of ads in a fixed loop, and each cohort starts the loop at a different point. Every cohort tests every ad evenly with no repeats, and staggered starts spread each ad's early tests across cohorts. The shade shows the round in which a cohort first tests each ad.

What the strategies do

Knows the cohort

  • Crossed rotation, then exploit. Test every cohort × ad pair evenly, then give each cohort its best ads.
  • Cohort bandit. Keep shifting each cohort's traffic toward the ads that are working for it.

Blind

  • Learn overall best. Shift everyone's traffic toward the ads that work best overall.
  • Follow the lead. The prisoners-puzzle idea. Choose the next ad from what converted after the previous ad, and whether it was clicked.

Rotate at random is the baseline. Perfect knowledge knows every true rate and is the upper limit. The model assumes a person converts at most once and clicks run at about four times the conversion rate. The same Python engine runs from the repo: python -m prisoners ads --help.

Every strategy runs on the same simulated audiences, so the differences come from the strategy alone.
