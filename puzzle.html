Test every channel at random, or follow the lead?

Each campaign team has one target segment and a budget to test half the channels. The launch only works if every team finds the channel that converts its segment. At random, the odds are about one in 1030. If each team follows the lead each test reveals, the odds rise to about 31%.

One launch, mapped by loop

Following the lead takes each team around the loop that contains its own channel. A team succeeds if that loop fits inside its test budget. One loop longer than the budget sinks every team on it at once.

How many teams hit, launch by launch

Each team has the same average odds under both strategies. Following the lead makes the outcomes move together: teams win as a group or lose as a group.

How the puzzle maps to marketing

prisonerA campaign team, one per audience segment
boxA channel the team can test
number in boxThe segment that channel actually converts
50 openingsEach team's test budget
loop strategyStart with the channel labelled for your segment. Its result names another segment, so test that segment's channel next, and keep going.
all go freeA launch that needs every segment covered

What it tells you: coordination doesn't make any single test better. It makes the outcomes correlated, and that correlation is worth a lot when success is all or nothing. It also makes the strategy fragile. A few percent of bad data breaks the chains, and the advantage disappears. Try the noise slider.
